Question 3

Which products tend to be bundled or bought together?

Why it matters

Products that reliably appear together are candidates for bundles, cross-sell prompts, and coordinated stocking. They also point to future directions for product marketing: which pairings to feature in promotions, catalogs and recommendations, and which product combinations are worth testing next.

Approach

Market-basket analysis over 830 orders: for every product pair seen together in at least 5 orders, compute lift (how much more often they co-occur than if bought independently) and confidence. "Chance" is the number of orders expected to contain both products if customers chose them independently: the share of orders with product A × the share with product B × the total number of orders. Lift is the actual number of shared orders divided by that expected number, so 1.0× means exactly as often as chance. A chi-square test checks whether each pair appears together more often than chance. The same lift is computed between product categories.

Main finding

One pairing stands well above the rest: Sir Rodney's Scones and Sirop d'érable appear together in 8 orders — 7.1× more than chance, and 33% of maple-syrup orders also include the scones (chi-square p < 0.001). Several cheese and dessert pairs (e.g. Gorgonzola Telino with Pavlova or Mozzarella) are 2–3× chance but rest on only 5–7 orders. At the category level every pair sits below 1.0: customers place focused orders rather than mixed baskets.

Business implication

Launch a scones-and-syrup bundle or cross-sell prompt — the evidence supports it. Treat the cheese/dessert pairs as hypotheses to test with a small promotion rather than as established patterns. Because orders are small and focused, broad cross-category bundling is unlikely to pay off.

Caveat: with 77 products and a median of 2 items per order, most pairs are rare, so lift estimates for low-count pairs are unstable. The chi-square test is also only approximate when counts are this small, so treat pairs with p-values near 0.05 as borderline.
