Discounts, sales reps, and product bundles at Northwind

Three questions a sales manager should be able to answer from Northwind's 830 orders and 2,155 order lines: whether discounts earn their cost, where sales performance really differs, and which products customers buy together. Each card below gives the short answer; open a tab for the full analysis, charts and data.

Question 1

Do discounted orders actually sell more product?

Yes, but it doesn't pay off in revenue. Compared with itself at full price, the typical product sells 25% more units per order line when discounted, yet net revenue per line shows no significant gain. Lift levels off above 15%, and the 10% tier shows none.

Implication: use discounts to move units, not to grow revenue; cap routine discounts at 15%.

Question 2

How does performance vary among the sales representatives?

Top reps win on volume, not deal size. Margaret Peacock leads with $232,891. Revenue gaps come from how many orders and customers each rep handles; average order values don't differ meaningfully. Most reps grew sharply by 1998, but two stayed flat.

Question 3

Which products tend to be bundled or bought together?

One reliable pair: scones and maple syrup. Sir Rodney's Scones and Sirop d'érable appear together 7.1× more often than chance, the only pair that holds up statistically. Orders are small and focused, and categories mix slightly less than chance.
